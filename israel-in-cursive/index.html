How to Write Israel in Cursive

Israel begins with a capital I, a tall stroke with a small loop at the bottom left that connects into the next letter from the baseline. Move into the s, a short pointed stroke that rises to the midline and closes back on itself before continuing along the baseline.

The join from s to r is the hard part of this word. Both letters have a small point at the midline, and if they are written too close together the pair looks like a squashed w. Give the s a clear exit along the baseline before rising into the r, and make the dip after the r's peak shallow so it flows into the a.

Finish with a-e-l: a closed oval, a small loop, and a tall looped l with an exit stroke. The l is the only ascender apart from the capital, and the word has no descenders, so Israel rises at both ends and stays low in the middle. Keep the height of the l equal to the height of the capital I.

Hebrew Cursive Is a Different Thing

In Hebrew the country is Yisrael, written ישראל. The romanised form converts in the generator; the Hebrew letters do not, since the Unicode script alphabets only cover A–Z. Hebrew is also written from right to left, so mixing a Hebrew word with Latin cursive on one line needs care in any design tool.

Hebrew does have a cursive form, called ktav yad or simply ktav, and it is what Israelis use for everyday handwriting. It looks quite different from the square printed letters, but unlike Latin cursive the letters are never joined to each other: each one stands alone. So "Israel in cursive" can mean two different things, the English word in flowing Latin script, which this page covers, or Hebrew handwriting, which uses separate rounded letters.

Uses for Israel in Cursive

Yom Ha'atzmaut, Israel's Independence Day, falls in April or May according to the Hebrew calendar. Cursive Israel also appears in travel captions from Jerusalem, Tel Aviv, and the Dead Sea, on bar and bat mitzvah invitations, in Birthright trip albums, on hummus and falafel restaurant menus, and on Middle East geography project covers.

The elegant script is the usual choice for invitations. Bold cursive suits captions. For designs that combine Hebrew and English, keep the Hebrew in a Hebrew font and use cursive only for the English line so the two scripts do not compete.

Israel in Cursive FAQ

How do you join the s to the r in Israel?

Give the s a clear exit stroke along the baseline before rising into the r, and keep the dip after the r shallow. Written too close together, the pair looks like a squashed w.

Is Hebrew cursive joined like English cursive?

No. Hebrew cursive, or ktav yad, uses rounded handwritten letter shapes, but each letter stands alone. The letters are never connected to one another.

Can I type Hebrew into the generator?

You can, but it will pass through unchanged. The Unicode script alphabets only cover A to Z, so use Israel or Yisrael in Latin letters for a styled result.

Does the capital I in Israel take a dot?

No. The cursive capital I is a tall stroke with a small loop at the bottom and takes no dot. Only lowercase i's are dotted, and Israel has none.

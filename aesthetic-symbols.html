What Aesthetic Symbols Are

Every symbol on this page is a Unicode character, the same kind of text as the letters you type. Hearts like ♡ and ❥ come from the Dingbats and Miscellaneous Symbols blocks; stars such as ✩ and ✧ are dingbats too; the ୨୧ bow is two Odia digits; ꒰ and ꒱ are Yi script brackets; ᡣ and 𐭩 come from Mongolian and Old Persian. None of it was designed as decoration — the internet adopted characters whose shapes happened to look pretty, and combos like ⋆｡°✩ and ˚ʚ♡ɞ˚ spread through Tumblr, Instagram, Discord and TikTok until they became a shared visual language.

Because they are text, aesthetic symbols work anywhere Unicode is supported and usually stay monochrome, taking the colour of the surrounding text. That is the main difference from emoji, which are drawn as coloured pictures by each platform and look different on iPhone, Android and desktop. When you want a soft, consistent look, text symbols are the safer choice; when you want colour, emoji are.

Aesthetic Symbols FAQ

What are aesthetic symbols?

Aesthetic symbols are Unicode characters — hearts, stars, sparkles, bows, moons, dividers and brackets — used to decorate bios, usernames, captions and notes. Because they are text, not images, they can be copied and pasted anywhere.

How do I copy an aesthetic symbol?

Click or tap any symbol on this page and it is copied to your clipboard instantly. It is also added to the collection box at the top, so you can gather several symbols and copy them together as one line.

What does ⋆｡°✩ mean?

It has no fixed meaning — it is a popular decorative combo of a star, a small circle, a degree sign and a sparkle that people put around names and headings for a dreamy, starry look.

What is the ୨୧ bow symbol?

୨୧ is two Odia script digits that together resemble a ribbon bow. It became the signature coquette-aesthetic symbol and is usually placed on both sides of a name or word.

Can I use aesthetic symbols in my Instagram or TikTok username?

Not in the @username, which most platforms limit to letters, numbers, periods and underscores. Symbols work in display names, bios, captions and comments instead.

Why do some symbols show as boxes or look different on my phone?

Each device draws Unicode from its own fonts. Rare characters may have no glyph on older systems and show as a box; emoji look different on iOS and Android by design. The plain text symbols here render on all modern phones.

Are these aesthetic symbols free?

Yes. Everything on this page is standard Unicode text, free to copy and use anywhere, with no sign-up.

How to Type in Cursive on Any Phone, Computer or App

You can type in cursive on any phone or computer, but not with a keyboard setting. Pasted Unicode cursive works in texts and profiles; a cursive font works in documents you control.

To type in cursive, you have two options. The first is to convert your words into Unicode cursive characters with a cursive text generator and paste them wherever you want them: a text message, an Instagram bio, a Discord name, a comment. The second is to switch a document you are editing, in Word, Google Docs or Canva, to a real cursive font. Both look like handwriting on screen, but they behave very differently once the text leaves your device.

Picking the wrong method is the reason most people give up. They set Word to a script font, copy the result into a text, and the other person sees plain letters. Or they paste Unicode cursive into a school report, and the spell checker marks every word red. This guide covers which method fits which job, then walks through the exact steps on each device and app.

Two ways to type in cursive, and when each one works

The difference comes down to what gets stored: the characters or the styling.

Unicode cursive changes the characters. Unicode, the standard every modern device uses for text, includes complete alphabets of script letters, mostly in its Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block. The letter 𝓪 is not an "a" in a fancy font; it is a separate character with its own code. Because the shape travels with the character, it stays cursive when you copy it into a message, a profile or a post. That is why this method works in places where you have no font menu at all.

A cursive font changes the styling. The letters stay ordinary "a", "b", "c", and the program draws them with a handwriting-style typeface. The look only exists inside that document or design. Paste the text somewhere else and it falls back to whatever font the destination uses.

How to type in cursive on your phone

Phone keyboards have no cursive setting, so on both iPhone and Android the copy-paste method does the work. The only differences are where the paste and shortcut options live.

iPhone and iPad

iOS does not let you change the system font or the keyboard's letters, so the copy-paste method is the practical route.

  1. Open Safari and go to the generator.
  2. Type your text, then tap copy on a style.
  3. Open Messages, Instagram, Notes or any other app, tap and hold the text field, and choose Paste.

If you type the same cursive phrase often, such as your name, save it as a shortcut. Go to Settings > General > Keyboard > Text Replacement, tap +, paste the cursive text into the Phrase field and pick a short Shortcut like "cname". From then on, typing the shortcut in any app offers the cursive version as a suggestion. It is the closest thing to a cursive key the iPhone has.

For a closer look at the iOS side, including what the font settings in Settings do and do not change, see our guide to getting a cursive font on iPhone.

Android

The steps are the same idea as on iPhone. Open Chrome or your usual browser, convert your text, tap copy, then long-press the text field in your app and tap Paste. Many Android keyboards also show recent clipboard items in a strip above the keys, which saves a step.

Gboard supports saved shortcuts too. In Gboard's settings, open Dictionary > Personal dictionary, choose your language, add the cursive text as a word and give it a short shortcut. Menu names vary a little between Android versions and phone makers, but the personal dictionary is where to look.

Android's font coverage depends on the phone maker and the system version. If a pasted style shows empty boxes, switch to Bold Cursive; our guide to cursive text that shows as boxes explains why this happens and how to fix it.

About cursive keyboard apps: app stores list keyboards that promise cursive letters as you go. They work by swapping your letters for the same Unicode characters a generator uses, and to do that they need full access to everything you type, including passwords and messages. If you only need cursive now and then, copying from a browser gives the same result without that permission.

How to text in cursive in messaging apps

When people ask how to text in cursive, they usually mean iMessage, WhatsApp, Messenger, Telegram or plain SMS. The answer is always the paste method, because a message only carries characters. Your phone's font choice never reaches the other person.

  • iMessage, WhatsApp, Telegram, Messenger: paste works well. These apps send full Unicode, and current phones display the script letters.
  • SMS to older phones: some carriers convert messages to a limited character set, and cursive can arrive as question marks. Keep anything important, such as times, addresses or codes, in plain text.
  • Group chats: not everyone is on the same device. A short cursive greeting is fine; a long cursive message may be unreadable for someone on an old phone or a smartwatch.

WhatsApp also has its own formatting: wrapping text in underscores makes it italic. That is slanted text, not cursive, and it only works inside WhatsApp. If a slant is all you want, an italic font generator gives you Unicode italic that works in other apps as well.

How to type in cursive on a computer

On a computer, the choice depends on the destination. For a browser-based chat, email or social post, use the generator and paste with Ctrl+V on Windows or Cmd+V on Mac, exactly as on a phone.

For documents, use a font. Both systems already ship with a few script typefaces:

  • Windows typically includes Segoe Script, Lucida Handwriting and Brush Script MT.
  • macOS includes Snell Roundhand, Apple Chancery, Bradley Hand and Zapfino.

To add another cursive font, download it from a source that states its license, then install it. On Windows, right-click the font file and choose Install. On a Mac, double-click the file and click Install Font in Font Book. It will appear in the font menu of most programs after you restart them. Our cursive fonts guide covers choosing and licensing script typefaces in more detail.

macOS also has a built-in way to insert single script characters: the Character Viewer, opened with Control+Command+Space, can find letters such as "script capital A" by name. It is fine for one initial, but far too slow for whole words.

How to type in cursive in Word and Google Docs

For school work, letters and anything you will edit later, a cursive font is the right tool. The words stay normal text, so spell check, find-and-replace, copy-paste into other documents and screen readers all keep working.

Microsoft Word

  1. Select the text you want in cursive, or place the cursor where you will start typing.
  2. Open the font menu on the Home tab and type the font name, for example "Segoe Script" or "Snell Roundhand".
  3. Type as normal. Every new letter appears in cursive.

If you will send the file to someone who might not have the same font, export it as a PDF so the look is preserved.

Google Docs

  1. Click the font menu in the toolbar and choose More fonts.
  2. Search for a script font such as Dancing Script, Great Vibes or Pacifico, and click it to add it to your list.
  3. Select your text and apply the font.

Google Docs fonts load from the web, so anyone who opens the document sees the same cursive, even on a computer without that font installed.

Canva and design tools

Canva, and most design apps, have a font picker with a large set of script typefaces. Search "script" or "handwriting" in the font list. Because the final design is exported as an image or PDF, the cursive looks the same for everyone. For formal pieces like invitations, print a test copy first, since thin script strokes can look lighter on paper than on screen.

Where typing in cursive does not work

Unicode cursive is flexible, but it is not ordinary text, and some places treat it that way.

  • Usernames and handles. Instagram, TikTok, X and most sites allow only letters, numbers and a few symbols in the handle. Put cursive in the display name or bio instead. The cursive text platform guide lists which fields accept it on each network.
  • Search. Search engines and in-app search treat 𝓢𝓪𝓶 and Sam as different words. A cursive-only name is harder for people to find.
  • Screen readers. Assistive technology may read each script letter by its full name, or skip it. Our post on whether cursive text is accessible covers safer ways to use it.
  • Forms. Email addresses, passwords, payment fields and official forms usually reject special characters. Never use cursive there.
  • Older devices. Smart TVs, car screens and older phones may show boxes instead of letters.

A good rule: use cursive for decoration and keep the information plain. A cursive name above a plain-text bio, or a cursive "Happy birthday" before a normal message, gets the look without costing clarity.

Tips for cursive typing that people can read

Handwriting on paper can be messy and still charming. On a screen, readability matters more, so a little restraint goes a long way.

  • Keep it short. Names, headings and a single line work best. Long passages in script slow readers down.
  • Mix sparingly. One cursive style per post or profile looks deliberate. Several styles at once looks cluttered.
  • Prefer bold for small sizes. Thin script strokes blur on small screens. The bold cursive generator focuses on the heavier styles that stay legible.
  • Check before you publish. Paste the text into a note, then view it on a second device or ask a friend for a screenshot.
  • Save your favorites. Keep your cursive name or signature line in a note or a keyboard shortcut so you do not have to regenerate it every time.

If what you actually want is to write in cursive by hand, rather than on a screen, that is a separate skill. The free cursive practice sheets are the place to start.

Quick answer: how do you type in cursive?

For anything you send or post, such as texts, bios, captions and display names, convert your words with the generator and paste them in. For anything you write and keep, such as essays, letters and designs, select a cursive font in Word, Google Docs or Canva. Keep usernames, contact details and form fields in plain text. Follow that split and you can type in cursive on any phone or computer without the text breaking on the other end.

Frequently asked questions

How do you type in cursive on a phone keyboard?

Phone keyboards cannot produce cursive letters directly. Type your text in a cursive text generator, tap copy on the style you like, then paste it into your message, bio or caption. The result is Unicode characters, so it stays cursive after you send it.

Can I make my whole keyboard type in cursive?

Some third-party keyboard apps offer font styles, but they work the same way as a generator: they swap your letters for Unicode lookalikes. They also ask for full keyboard access, which lets the app see everything you type. Copying from a web generator avoids that permission.

Why does changing the font to cursive not work when I text someone?

A font setting only changes how text looks on your own screen or inside your own document. Text messages carry the characters, not the font, so the other person sees their default font. To text in cursive, you have to send cursive Unicode characters instead.

Is it okay to type in cursive for a username?

Most platforms reject special characters in usernames and handles, and cursive usernames are hard for others to search or type. Keep the handle plain and put the cursive version in the display name or bio, where it is allowed and still readable.

What is the best cursive font for Word or Google Docs?

For readable documents, pick a clear connected script rather than a heavily looped one, and use it only for headings, names or short lines. Google Docs includes several script fonts under More fonts; in Word, any cursive font installed on your computer will appear in the font menu.

How to Write Germany in Cursive

Germany begins with a capital G, a tall open curve that drops to the baseline and finishes with a small loop. In most school styles that loop connects into the lowercase e; if you prefer to lift the pen, do so consistently. The e is a small closed loop.

Next comes the join that gives most writers trouble: r into m. The r rises to a small point, dips, and then must flow straight into the first hump of the m. If the dip after the r is too deep, the m looks like it has four humps. Keep the dip shallow and start the m from the midline. The m itself has three humps, evenly spaced.

Finish with a-n-y. The a is a closed oval, the n has two humps, and the y drops below the baseline into a loop and returns with an exit stroke. The y is the only descender in the word and the only stroke apart from the capital that leaves the midline band, so make its loop clean and match its depth to any other descenders on the page.

Germany or Deutschland in Cursive

The German name for the country is Deutschland, which converts fully in the generator because it has no umlauts. Words that do carry umlauts, such as München or Köln, will show those letters in plain form. Deutschland is eleven letters long with three ascenders, so it needs more horizontal space than Germany but looks impressive at large sizes.

Germany has an unusually rich handwriting history. For most of the last two centuries Germans wrote a completely different cursive, Kurrent, and later Sütterlin, which look nothing like the Latin cursive on this page. That history belongs to the German in cursive page; here the focus is the English country name.

Where Germany in Cursive Is Used

German Unity Day on October 3 is the national holiday, and Oktoberfest in Munich runs from late September. Cursive Germany also suits travel captions from Berlin, the Rhine, and Bavaria, beer-garden and bakery signage, Christmas-market posts, and German-class or European-history project covers.

Bold cursive is the safest choice for captions. The decorative fraktur-style option, unusually, is a genuinely good match here: blackletter typefaces were the standard German printing style until the 1940s, so the heavy decorative script fits Oktoberfest and Christmas-market themes better than it fits most other countries.

Germany in Cursive FAQ

How do you join the r to the m in Germany?

Bring the r up to a small point at the midline, make only a shallow dip, and start the first hump of the m from the midline. A deep dip makes the m appear to have an extra hump.

Does Germany have any descenders in cursive?

One. The final y drops below the baseline into a loop. Every other lowercase letter in the word stays between the baseline and the midline.

Can I write Deutschland in cursive with this tool?

Yes. Deutschland uses only plain letters and converts fully in all three styles. Words with umlauts, such as München, keep those letters in plain form.

Is the decorative style a good fit for Germany?

Yes, more than for most countries. Blackletter type was the standard German printing style until the 1940s, so the heavy decorative script suits Oktoberfest and Christmas-market designs well.

How to Write Egypt in Cursive

Egypt starts with a capital E, which in cursive is a double curve, like a rounded numeral 3, finishing at the baseline. It is one of the connecting capitals, so move straight into the g.

Then come three descenders in a row: g, y, p. The g is a closed oval followed by a loop below the line. The y is a cup followed by a loop below the line. The p is a straight stroke below the line followed by a small bowl. The g and y loops should be identical in size and shape, and the p stroke should reach the same depth. Space the three letters a little wider than usual so the loops do not overlap.

Finish with the t, a short ascender crossed after the word is done. Egypt is unusual in having its only ascender at the very end and all its depth in the middle, so it looks bottom-heavy by design. Leave extra line spacing below it so the three loops do not collide with the next line.

Egypt Invented Cursive Writing

Egypt has a stronger claim on the word cursive than any other country on this site. Ancient Egyptian scribes developed hieratic, a flowing, simplified form of hieroglyphs written quickly with a reed pen on papyrus, more than four thousand years ago. It was followed by demotic, an even faster script that appears on the Rosetta Stone alongside hieroglyphs and Greek. Both were cursive in the true sense: shapes simplified and joined for speed.

The modern Arabic name for the country is Misr (مصر), and it converts in its romanised form but not in Arabic script, since the generator only styles A–Z. Arabic itself is inherently cursive, with letters that change shape depending on where they sit in a word, which is another reason Egypt and cursive writing have such a long shared history.

Egypt in Cursive FAQ

How do you write g, y, and p in a row in cursive Egypt?

Make the g and y loops identical in size and shape, take the p stroke to the same depth, and space the three letters slightly wider than usual so the loops do not overlap. Leave extra line spacing below the word.

Does the capital E in Egypt join the g?

Yes. The cursive capital E is a double curve that finishes at the baseline and connects directly into the lowercase g.

What was the first cursive script in Egypt?

Hieratic, a simplified flowing form of hieroglyphs written with a reed pen on papyrus more than four thousand years ago. It was later followed by the even faster demotic script.

Can I write Egypt in Arabic with this generator?

No. Arabic script passes through unchanged because the Unicode script alphabets only cover A to Z. The romanised name Misr converts fully.

How to Write 6 (Six) in Cursive

Begin at the upper area and draw a long, curving stroke that sweeps down and to the left in a gentle arc, then curls right and closes into a circle at the bottom. The key is the inward-closing loop at the base — the pen travels all the way down and then loops back up and right to close the circle, rather than leaving a gap.

Stroke Steps

  1. Start at approximately 1 o'clock on the upper part
  2. Curve down-left in a generous arc
  3. At the lower area, begin curling right and upward
  4. Close the loop by connecting back to the lower portion of the downstroke

6 in Print vs Cursive

Print 6 often has a sharp angle where the stem meets the circle. Cursive 6 makes this a continuous flowing curve with no angle — the stem and circle are one fluid stroke. The result is rounder and more compact.

FAQ — 6 in Cursive

How do you write 6 in cursive?

Start at the upper-right, sweep down-left in a broad curve, then curl right and close into a circle at the bottom. The entire digit is a single continuous stroke — the downward sweep transitions smoothly into the closing loop.

What does cursive 6 look like?

Cursive 6 is a continuous curved stroke with a broad sweep at the top and a closed loop at the bottom. It looks more rounded and fluid than the printed version.

Is 6 hard to write in cursive?

The closing loop at the bottom requires the pen to change direction mid-stroke, which can be tricky at first. Focus on making the inward curl smooth — abrupt direction changes are the most common beginner mistake.

How does cursive 6 differ from cursive 9?

Cursive 6 has its loop at the bottom and an open top. Cursive 9 has its loop at the top and a descending tail below. They are essentially mirror shapes in vertical orientation.

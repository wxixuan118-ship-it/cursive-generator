How to Write 1 (One) in Cursive

Begin with a small upward entry stroke at the upper-left. Then draw a firm, straight vertical downstroke to the baseline. Many cursive writers add a short baseline serif or foot stroke at the bottom. The entry stroke is the key difference from the printed numeral.

Stroke Steps

  1. Make a short upward-left entry stroke (like a small 'v' on its side)
  2. Without lifting the pen, pivot and draw a straight vertical downstroke
  3. Optionally add a short rightward foot at the baseline for legibility
  4. The result looks like a sail shape — an angled cap with a vertical mast

1 in Print vs Cursive

Printed 1 is usually just a vertical line. Cursive 1 adds a distinctive entry stroke at the top-left, giving it more personality and helping it connect naturally with adjacent letters in a flowing word.

FAQ — 1 in Cursive

How do you write 1 in cursive?

Start with a small upward-left entry stroke, then pivot down into a straight vertical downstroke. Many writers add a baseline foot. The entry stroke at the top is the main difference from a printed 1.

What makes cursive 1 different from print 1?

The printed 1 is typically a plain vertical line. The cursive 1 adds a small entry stroke at the upper-left that gives it a characteristic appearance and helps it flow naturally when written alongside other cursive letters.

Is 1 easy to write in cursive?

Yes. Cursive 1 is one of the simplest digits — two strokes (entry + downstroke) or three if you add a baseline foot. The main challenge is keeping the vertical stroke consistently straight.

Does the number 1 look like a lowercase cursive letter?

The cursive 1 can resemble a lowercase 'l' in some handwriting styles. To avoid confusion in written work, some people write the digit 1 with a larger entry stroke or a more pronounced baseline foot to distinguish it.

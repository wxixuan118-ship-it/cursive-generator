How to Write 4 (Four) in Cursive

Cursive 4 is typically written in two strokes. First, draw a diagonal line down-left from upper-right to the midpoint. Second, draw the vertical downstroke from the top through the midpoint to the baseline — crossing the first stroke — with an optional short rightward connector at the base. Alternatively, some cursive styles connect the two strokes into one fluid motion.

Stroke Steps

  1. Draw a diagonal stroke from upper-right to the mid-left (the 'flag')
  2. Lift the pen and place it at the top-centre
  3. Draw a straight vertical downstroke to the baseline
  4. The vertical stroke crosses the diagonal — add a short rightward base for connecting cursive

4 in Print vs Cursive

Print 4 has a clear angular crossbar. Cursive 4 in many styles keeps a similar two-stroke structure but softens the angles. Some joined cursive styles write it as a single connected stroke, integrating the crossbar into a flowing loop.

FAQ — 4 in Cursive

How do you write 4 in cursive?

Typically in two strokes: first a diagonal flag stroke from upper-right to the mid-left, then a vertical downstroke from the top that crosses the flag. Some cursive styles join these into a single looping stroke.

Does 4 change a lot in cursive?

Four is one of the digits that varies most between handwriting styles. Some writers use a traditional two-stroke form; others use a single-stroke looping version. Both are considered valid cursive.

Why is 4 harder in cursive?

The crossing stroke at the midpoint requires precise pen placement. The diagonal and vertical strokes must cross at exactly the right point, which takes spatial practice.

What is the easiest way to write 4 in cursive?

Start with the diagonal stroke (upper-right to mid-left), then the vertical downstroke crossing it. Keep the crossing at roughly the midpoint height. A consistent diagonal angle makes it easier to find the crossing point reliably.

How to Write 8 (Eight) in Cursive

Begin at the centre and draw a clockwise loop upward to form the upper oval — smaller than the lower. Without lifting the pen, cross back through the centre point and continue into the larger lower oval, drawing it counterclockwise to close at the centre. The cross at the centre is the defining feature of cursive 8.

Stroke Steps

  1. Place pen at the centre-crossing point
  2. Loop upward-right clockwise to form the small upper oval
  3. Cross back through the centre
  4. Continue downward-right counterclockwise to form the larger lower oval
  5. Close the lower oval at the centre crossing point

8 in Print vs Cursive

Print 8 can be drawn as two separate circles stacked and connected. Cursive 8 is a single fluid figure-eight stroke with a natural crossing point at the centre. The upper oval is noticeably smaller than the lower one in cursive.

FAQ — 8 in Cursive

How do you write 8 in cursive?

Start at the centre crossing point, loop clockwise to form a small upper oval, cross back through the centre, then loop counterclockwise to form a larger lower oval, closing at the centre. It is one continuous stroke.

What is the trick to writing 8 in cursive?

Start from the centre, not the top. Beginning at the crossing point means both ovals flow naturally from that anchor, making the crossing automatic. Starting from the top often leads to an uneven crossing point.

Is 8 hard to write in cursive?

Moderately — the cross at the centre requires good pen control, and keeping the two ovals proportionate takes practice. Starting from the centre (rather than the top) is the most common expert tip.

Does cursive 8 look like an infinity symbol?

Yes — cursive 8 is essentially a vertical figure-eight, the same shape as the infinity symbol (∞) rotated 90°. The flowing, single-stroke nature of cursive 8 makes the resemblance especially strong.

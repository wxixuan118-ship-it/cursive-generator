How to Write 3 (Three) in Cursive

Begin at the upper-right and draw the top arc — a rightward-facing curve that comes to a centre point at the middle. Without lifting the pen, continue from that centre point into a second, slightly larger rightward-facing arc that curves down to the baseline. The two arcs stack vertically with a shared middle pivot.

Stroke Steps

  1. Start at the upper-left and curve right to form the top arc
  2. At the centre point, change direction for the lower arc
  3. Draw a larger rightward arc curving down to the baseline
  4. The baseline ends with a slight leftward curl in joined cursive

3 in Print vs Cursive

Print 3 can have sharp angular joins between its two arcs. Cursive 3 makes both arcs flow from a single continuous stroke, with a smooth pivot at the midpoint. The overall shape is rounder and more organic than the printed version.

FAQ — 3 in Cursive

How do you write 3 in cursive?

Draw two rightward-facing arcs in a single continuous stroke — a smaller one on top and a slightly larger one below. The two arcs share a centre pivot point. End with a slight leftward curl at the baseline.

What does a cursive 3 look like?

Cursive 3 looks like a backwards rounded 'E' or two stacked bumps facing right. It is rounder and more fluid than the printed version, with a smooth transition between the two arcs.

Is 3 hard to write in cursive?

Moderately easy. The two-arc shape is straightforward to understand, but keeping both arcs proportionate and the transition smooth takes some practice.

How does 3 connect to other cursive numbers?

Cursive 3 ends with a slight leftward curl at the baseline. In joined cursive, some writers lift slightly before the next digit; others use the tail of the 3 as an entry stroke into the following character.

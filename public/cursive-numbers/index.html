How to Write Numbers in Cursive

Cursive numbers use the same basic stroke logic as cursive letters — fluid, connected movements that minimise pen lifts. Most digits look similar to their printed counterparts but with softer angles, subtle lean, and connecting strokes that let numbers integrate naturally into cursive words and sentences.

Click any number above for a full stroke guide, step-by-step instructions, and examples. The cursive generator on each page lets you type phrases containing that digit to see how it looks alongside cursive letters in real time.

Which Numbers Change the Most in Cursive?

Least changed: 0 (still an oval), 1 (gains an entry stroke), 7 (gains a crossbar in many traditions).

Most changed: 2 (angular base becomes a flowing S-curve), 5 (body drawn before cap), 8 (always a single crossing stroke in cursive), and 9 (loop and tail become one continuous stroke).

Connecting Numbers to Cursive Letters

When writing cursive words that include numbers — dates, addresses, measurements — most digits end with a rightward connecting stroke that naturally leads into the next character. The exceptions are digits like 4 and 7 where the tail points downward-left; these typically require a small pen lift before the following letter.

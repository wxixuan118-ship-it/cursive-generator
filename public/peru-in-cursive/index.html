Enter Peru below and copy Peru in cursive in three styles. Peru is one of the shortest country names on this site, but two of its four letters, the capital P and the lowercase r, are among the hardest cursive shapes to get right. The guide below takes them one at a time.

How to Write Peru in Cursive

Begin with the capital P. Like the cursive capital B, the P does not connect to what follows: its bowl closes back on the stem, so you lift the pen and start the e beside it. Place the e close enough that the gap is smaller than the space you would leave between words.

The e is a small loop, and from its exit stroke you go into the r. The cursive r rises to a small point at the midline, dips slightly, and then continues into the next letter. The most common mistake is making it look like an undotted i; the dip after the peak is what makes it an r. Finally, write the u as two cups joined at the baseline, with a short exit stroke.

With only four letters, Peru rewards careful proportion. The capital should be about twice the height of the lowercase letters, and the three lowercase letters should sit at exactly the same height. Because the word is short, any inconsistency is obvious, which is what makes it a useful practice word.

Peru or Perú? Handling the Accent

The Spanish spelling is Perú, with an acute accent on the u. In the generator that accented ú stays in its normal form while the P, e, and r convert, because the Unicode script alphabets have no accented letters. For a fully cursive result use the English spelling. In handwriting the accent is simply a short stroke added above the u after the word is finished, in the same pass as any t-crosses or i-dots.

Related words behave the same way: peruano converts fully, but Fiestas Patrias converts while Día de la Independencia keeps its plain í. When building a bilingual design, test each phrase in the tool before deciding on a layout.

Where Peru in Cursive Is Used

The main date is Fiestas Patrias on July 28 and 29, Peru's independence celebration. Cursive Peru also suits travel captions from Machu Picchu, Cusco, Lima, and the Sacred Valley, restaurant menus featuring ceviche or lomo saltado, and Inca-history or South America project titles at school.

Short words work in every style, including the decorative one, because there are only four letters to read. This is one of the few country names where the fraktur-style option remains readable even in a caption.

Peru in Cursive FAQ

Does the capital P in Peru join to the e?

No. The cursive capital P closes on its own stem, so you lift the pen after it and start the lowercase e just to the right, leaving a gap smaller than a word space.

How do you make the r in Peru look like an r and not an i?

Bring the stroke up to a small point at the midline, then add a slight dip before continuing into the u. That dip after the peak is what distinguishes a cursive r from an undotted i.

Will Perú with an accent convert to cursive?

Only partly. The P, e, and r convert, but the accented ú has no script-style equivalent and stays in its normal form. Use Peru without the accent for a fully styled word.

Is Peru short enough for the decorative style in a caption?

Yes. With only four letters the decorative style stays readable even at caption size, which is not true of longer names.

About Cursive Text Generator

Cursive Text Generator is a free, browser-based tool that turns plain text into cursive and other decorative Unicode styles you can copy and paste anywhere: Instagram bios, Discord names, TikTok captions, signatures, invitations and more.

Why This Site Exists

The site started with a simple annoyance: every time we wanted a cursive name for a profile, the tools we found were slow, plastered with pop-ups, and never explained why the text turned into boxes on someone else's phone. So we built the version we wanted to use. Type once, compare a few dozen styles side by side, copy the one you like, and get a straight answer about where it will and will not display.

It has since grown into a collection of tools around the same idea: a cursive text generator, signature styles, cursive fonts, an A–Z cursive alphabet, printable practice sheets for learners, and a growing set of themed generators. We also release our own fonts, such as CTG Everly Script, under an open licence.

Who Makes It

Cursive Text Generator is built and maintained by a small independent team led by Philip, a web developer with a long-standing interest in typography and handwriting. There is no large company behind the site. We write the guides ourselves, test the styles on real phones, and fix things when readers tell us a character is misbehaving on their device.

How the Site Works

Everything runs in your browser. The generators convert your text with JavaScript on your device, so nothing you type is sent to us. No login, no download, no limits. The site is funded by advertising, which is what keeps every tool free; how that works is explained in our Privacy Policy.

Editorial Standards

Our guides exist to answer the practical questions people actually have: which styles survive copy and paste, why some characters show as boxes, how to pick a signature style, how to use practice sheets with a child. We test claims on current iOS, Android, Windows and macOS before publishing, cite official documentation where it exists, and update articles when platforms change. If something is wrong, we want to hear about it.

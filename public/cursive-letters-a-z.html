Capital vs Lowercase Cursive Letters

The two cases behave differently in cursive, and it is not just a size difference. Cursive capitals are largely decorative and several of them — A, D, F, G, O, Q, S, T, V, W — do not connect forward to the letter after them at all, which is why a cursive name often shows a visible gap after its first letter. Lowercase letters are built to connect: each one has an entry stroke and an exit stroke, and the exit height is what decides how the next letter starts.

That is the detail worth knowing if you are writing by hand. Most lowercase letters hand off at the baseline, but w, o, v, and b hand off from the top of the x-height instead, so the letter that follows has to be entered from above.

Which Cursive Style Should You Copy?

The letters in the index above are Cursive Script, the most letter-like of the five styles this site generates. The others suit different jobs:

  • Cursive Script 𝒜𝒶 — closest to handwriting. The default for bios, names, and captions.
  • Bold Cursive 𝓐𝓪 — same shapes with more weight. Holds up at small sizes where plain script turns to mush.
  • Old Style Script 𝔄𝔞 — Fraktur, not cursive. Reads as gothic; good for logos, wrong for anything meant to look handwritten.
  • Double-Struck 𝔸𝕒 and Clean Monospace 𝙰𝚊 — decorative, not cursive at all. Listed here because people usually want to compare them side by side.

Where Cursive Letters Work — and Where They Break

Because these are Unicode characters rather than a font, they carry into places that never let you change the typeface: Instagram bios, TikTok display names, Discord nicknames, YouTube titles, spreadsheet cells. Three things to watch for:

  • Search and sorting. A cursive name will not match a plain-text search for the same word, and it sorts unpredictably in lists.
  • Screen readers. Most read these characters out letter by letter, or skip them. Never use them for information a reader actually needs.
  • Older devices. A system without a font covering this Unicode range shows empty boxes. Current iOS, Android, Windows, and macOS are fine; some older Android builds are not.

Cursive Letters A–Z FAQ

What are cursive letters A to Z?

In handwriting, they are the 26 letters of the alphabet written in a joined-up script where each letter flows into the next. Online, “cursive letters” almost always means the Unicode characters shown above — a set of symbols shaped like script handwriting that can be copied into any text field.

Can I copy and paste individual cursive letters?

Yes. Click any letter in the grid and the capital and lowercase pair goes to your clipboard as real text, not an image. Paste it anywhere that accepts typing.

Why do a few cursive letters look slightly different from the rest?

Eight capitals (B, E, F, H, I, L, M, R) and three lowercase letters (e, g, o) are not part of the Mathematical Script block that supplies the other characters. Unicode had already encoded those eleven separately in the Letterlike Symbols block years earlier, so the script block was given holes where they would have gone. They are drawn by a different part of the font, which is why their weight or slant can look a shade off next to their neighbours. Nothing is broken — it is a quirk of how Unicode grew.

Are these a real cursive font?

No. They are characters, not a typeface, which is exactly why they survive being pasted into apps that lock the font. If you need an installable file for print or design software, see cursive fonts.

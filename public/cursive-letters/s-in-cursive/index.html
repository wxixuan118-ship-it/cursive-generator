Cursive S Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital S in Cursive

  1. Start near cap height with a small hook curling left.
  2. Sweep left and down into the upper curve.
  3. Reverse into the lower curve, travelling right then left.
  4. Finish with a small loop resting on the baseline.

The capital S does not join forward, so a gap after it is correct.

How to Write a Lowercase s in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and rise diagonally to a point at x-height.
  2. Curve back down and to the left.
  3. Close a small bowl against the baseline.
  4. Exit right along the baseline.

The pointed top and the closed bottom bowl are both required — this is the letter where cursive departs furthest from the printed form after z.

Cursive s vs Cursive r

Both rise from the baseline into a feature at x-height and drop back down, and in quick handwriting the two tops converge. The r has a flat shoulder that runs right; the s comes to a point and then turns back left into a closed bowl. If the bowl does not close, the s loses its lower half and reads as an r, which is how sun and run get confused.

Joining s to the Next Letter

The s exits at the baseline from the bottom of its bowl, so it joins forward cleanly. The awkward part is that the exit leaves from the right side of a closed shape, so an s followed by another round letter — so, sa — can crowd. A little extra space after the s keeps both bowls readable.

Common Mistakes with Cursive S

  • An open bottom bowl. Without it the s is an r.
  • A rounded top. The s comes to a point at x-height.
  • Writing a printed s. The printed zigzag breaks the flow of joined-up text.
  • A bowl that dips below the baseline. The s sits entirely inside the x-height.

Cursive S FAQ

What is the cursive S?

The cursive S is the joined-up form of the letter S. The capital is a tall double curve finishing in a small loop on the baseline and does not join forward; the lowercase rises to a point at x-height and closes into a small bowl on the baseline. Online, “cursive S” also means the copy-and-paste characters (𝒮 and 𝓈) above.

How do you write s in cursive?

In short: rise diagonally from the baseline to a point at x-height, curve back down to the left, close a small bowl on the baseline and exit right. Steps and the diagram are above.

Why does my cursive s look like an r?

The bottom bowl is not closing. An s with an open bottom is just a rise and a fall, which is what an r is. Close the bowl against the baseline.

Why does a cursive s look so different from a printed s?

Because it is built from a rise, a reversal and a closed bowl rather than a zigzag. After z, s is the letter where the cursive and printed forms diverge most, and writing the printed version inside joined-up text is a common giveaway.

Cursive K Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital K in Cursive

  1. Start below cap height with a small curl and pull the stem to the baseline.
  2. Finish it with a flick to the right.
  3. Bring a diagonal down from cap height to meet the stem near x-height.
  4. Push the second diagonal out and down to the baseline.

Both diagonals meet the stem at the same point. Meeting at different heights is what makes a capital K look unbalanced.

How to Write a Lowercase k in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and carry a stroke up to the ascender line.
  2. Come straight back down to the baseline.
  3. Push out to the right into a small loop at x-height.
  4. Kick the leg down to the baseline and exit right.

The little loop where the leg meets the stem is the k's signature — without it the letter is an h.

Cursive k vs Cursive h

Both begin with the same full-height stem and both end on the baseline, so the reader is looking at the middle. An h pushes into a smooth open arch; a k makes a small loop against the stem and then kicks a straight leg out to the baseline. Smooth the loop away in fast writing and the k becomes an h, which is how like can read as lihe.

Joining k to the Next Letter

The leg lands on the baseline, so the k joins forward without difficulty. Entering it after a high-exiting letter is the awkward case — in book or oak you drop into the top of the tall stem from above rather than rising into it, and the stem tends to come out short.

Common Mistakes with Cursive K

  • No loop. A k without its loop is an h.
  • A leg that stops above the baseline. The leg has to land on the line to carry the join.
  • A short stem. The k stem is a full ascender, like b, h and l.
  • An oversized loop. Too large and the letter reads as a b with a tail.

Cursive K FAQ

What is the cursive K?

The cursive K is the joined-up form of the letter K. The capital is a full-height stem with two diagonals meeting it at x-height; the lowercase is a tall stem with a small loop and a kicked leg attached at x-height. Online, “cursive K” also means the copy-and-paste characters (𝒦 and 𝓀) above.

How do you write k in cursive?

In short: up to the ascender, straight back down to the baseline, push out into a small loop at x-height, then kick the leg down to the baseline and exit. Steps and the diagram are above.

Why does my cursive k look like an h?

The loop has been smoothed into an arch. The loop against the stem, plus the straight leg that follows it, is the only thing separating k from h — both share the same tall stem and baseline exit.

Does the cursive k have a loop?

Yes, in most copybooks. A small loop sits where the leg meets the stem at x-height, and it is the letter's distinguishing feature.

Can I copy and paste a cursive K?

Cursive Z Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital Z in Cursive

  1. Start near cap height with a small hook and draw the top bar to the right.
  2. Bring the stroke down and left in a long diagonal.
  3. Land on the baseline.
  4. Carry the base stroke out to the right to finish.

Some copybooks give the capital Z a descender loop as well. Both forms are taught; the flat-based version shown here is the more common.

How to Write a Lowercase z in Cursive

  1. Start at x-height with a small arch to the right.
  2. Cut back down and left in a diagonal to the baseline.
  3. Continue below the baseline into a small loop.
  4. Bring the loop back up to the baseline to exit.

This is the letter where cursive and print diverge most: the printed z has no descender at all, the cursive z does.

Cursive z vs Printed z

No other letter changes as much between the two systems. A printed z is a flat zigzag sitting entirely on the baseline; the cursive z has a rounded top and drops a loop below the line. People who learned to print first almost always write a printed z inside otherwise joined-up handwriting, which is one of the easiest tells that someone was taught cursive late or not at all.

Joining z to the Next Letter

The descender loop comes back up to the baseline, so the z joins forward like any other looped descender. In practice this rarely matters — z is uncommon in English and most often sits at the end of a word or doubled, as in buzz or jazz, where the second z simply repeats the loop.

Common Mistakes with Cursive Z

  • Writing a printed z. The flat zigzag breaks the flow of joined-up text.
  • No descender loop. The cursive z drops below the line.
  • A loop that swings right. The z loop goes left, like a g.
  • A pointed top. The cursive z begins with a small rounded arch, not a corner.

Cursive Z FAQ

What is the cursive Z?

The cursive Z is the joined-up form of the letter Z. The capital is a top bar with a diagonal down to a base stroke; the lowercase has a rounded top, a diagonal to the baseline and a loop dropping below the line. Online, “cursive Z” also means the copy-and-paste characters (𝒵 and 𝓏) above.

How do you write z in cursive?

In short: a small arch at x-height, a diagonal cutting down and left to the baseline, then a loop below the line that comes back up to exit. Steps and diagram are above.

Why does a cursive z look so different from a printed z?

Because it gains a descender. The printed z is a flat zigzag on the baseline; the cursive z has a rounded top and loops below the line. It is the letter where the two systems diverge most, and writing the printed form inside cursive is a common giveaway.

Does the cursive capital Z have a tail?

Both forms are taught. Some copybooks finish the capital Z with a descender loop, others with a flat base stroke. The flat-based version shown above is the more common in modern teaching.

Can I copy and paste a cursive Z?

Yes. Note that the Old Style Script capital (ℨ) and the Double-Struck capital (ℤ) both come from the Letterlike Symbols block rather than their own blocks. They paste like any other character.

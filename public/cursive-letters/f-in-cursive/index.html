Cursive F Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital F in Cursive

  1. Start left of centre near cap height and sweep a wide curve to the right.
  2. Bring the stroke back left and down into the stem.
  3. Pull the stem to the baseline and finish with a small flick right.
  4. Add the crossbar at x-height last, left to right.

The capital F does not join forward. Whatever follows it starts with its own entry stroke.

How to Write a Lowercase f in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and carry a long stroke up to the ascender line.
  2. Loop over at the top and bring the stroke back down, crossing your upstroke near x-height.
  3. Continue below the baseline into the descender and loop left.
  4. Come back up to the baseline to exit. Add the crossbar last.

The f is the only letter in the lowercase alphabet that occupies the ascender and the descender at once, which is why it needs more vertical room than anything else you write.

Cursive f vs Cursive t

Both are tall letters with a crossbar, and at speed they collapse into each other. The difference is entirely below the baseline: the f carries a descender loop, the t stops dead on the baseline. The second difference is height — the t reaches only about three quarters of the ascender, while the f goes the whole way.

Joining f to the Next Letter

The f exits from the bottom loop back up at the baseline, so the next letter joins normally. The awkward part is spacing, not connection: because the letter is so tall and so deep, a cramped f collides with the line above and below. In practice this is the letter that forces you to write larger.

Common Mistakes with Cursive F

  • No descender loop. Without it the f is a t.
  • Crossing too high. The upstroke and downstroke should cross near x-height, not up at the ascender.
  • Adding the crossbar mid-letter. Finish the whole stroke first, then cross.
  • A cramped loop. Both loops need to be open or the letter fills in as a blot.

Cursive F FAQ

What is the cursive F?

The cursive F is the joined-up form of the letter F. The capital is a wide top curve over a stem with a crossbar at x-height and does not connect forward. The lowercase is unique: one continuous stroke from the ascender line down through a descender loop, crossed near x-height. Online, “cursive F” also means the copy-and-paste characters (ℱ and 𝒻) above.

How do you write f in cursive?

In short: up to the ascender, loop over, back down crossing your own upstroke at x-height, on below the baseline into a descender loop, then back up to the baseline. The crossbar goes on last. Full steps and the diagram are above.

Why is the cursive f so hard?

Because it is the only lowercase letter that uses the ascender and the descender in one stroke, so it needs nearly twice the vertical space of a normal letter and has two loops to keep open. On narrow-ruled paper it is the first letter to collide with the lines above and below.

What is the difference between a cursive f and a cursive t?

The descender. The f loops below the baseline; the t stops on it. The f is also taller — full ascender height against roughly three quarters for the t.

Can I copy and paste a cursive F?

Yes. Note that the Cursive Script capital (ℱ) comes from the Letterlike Symbols block rather than the Mathematical Script block, because Unicode encoded it there first. It pastes like any other character.

Cursive R Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital R in Cursive

  1. Start below cap height with a small entry curl and pull the stem to the baseline.
  2. Return to the top and sweep the bowl out to the right.
  3. Close the bowl on the stem at about x-height.
  4. From that join, kick the leg out and down to the baseline.

The leg starts where the bowl closes on the stem, not from the stem itself. That join is what separates a capital R from a capital P.

How to Write a Lowercase r in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and rise to just above x-height.
  2. Flatten into a short horizontal shoulder running right.
  3. Drop from the end of the shoulder down towards the baseline.
  4. Exit right along the baseline.

The flat shoulder is the whole letter. Round it and you have written an n.

Cursive r vs Cursive n

Both rise from the baseline and come back down to it, and both sit inside the x-height, so the top does all the work. An n makes a rounded arch; an r makes a short flat shoulder that overshoots x-height slightly before dropping. Writers who round the shoulder produce a letter that reads as n, which is why run can come out looking like nun.

Joining r to the Next Letter

The r exits on the baseline and joins forward easily. The difficult sequence is rn, as in corner or turn: a rounded r followed by an n gives two near-identical arches, and the pair is frequently misread as an m. Keeping the r's shoulder flat is the only reliable fix.

Common Mistakes with Cursive R

  • A rounded shoulder. That makes it an n.
  • A shoulder that is too long. It becomes a v or a stray connector.
  • Not overshooting x-height. The r's shoulder sits slightly above the line, which is part of how it is recognised.
  • Confusing the capital's leg origin. The leg leaves the bowl join, not the stem.

Cursive R FAQ

What is the cursive R?

The cursive R is the joined-up form of the letter R. The capital is a stem with a bowl on its upper right and a leg kicking out from where the bowl closes; the lowercase is a short stroke with a flat shoulder just above x-height, dropping to the baseline. Online, “cursive R” also means the copy-and-paste characters (ℛ and 𝓇) above.

How do you write r in cursive?

In short: rise from the baseline to just above x-height, flatten into a short shoulder running right, then drop back to the baseline and exit. The flat shoulder is the letter. Steps and the diagram are above.

Why does my cursive r look like an n?

You are rounding the shoulder into an arch. An r's top is flat and slightly above x-height; an n's is a rounded arch at x-height. It is the only difference between them.

Why is ‘rn’ hard to read in cursive?

Because a rounded r followed by an n produces two matching arches, which the eye reads as a single m. Words like corner and modern are the usual casualties. Keep the r's shoulder flat.

Can I copy and paste a cursive R?

Yes. Note that the Cursive Script, Old Style Script and Double-Struck capitals (ℛ, ℜ and ℝ) all come from the Letterlike Symbols block rather than their own — R is one of the few letters where three of the five styles are drawn from elsewhere. They paste normally.

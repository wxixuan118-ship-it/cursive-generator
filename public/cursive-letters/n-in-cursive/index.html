Cursive N Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital N in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and carry a stroke up to cap height.
  2. Come back down the same line to the baseline.
  3. Run a diagonal up to cap height on the right.
  4. Drop the second stem to the baseline and exit right.

The cursive capital N keeps the diagonal of its printed form, which makes it one of the more recognisable capitals.

How to Write a Lowercase n in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and rise to x-height.
  2. Come straight down to the baseline.
  3. Push up into one arch that touches x-height.
  4. Bring it down and exit along the baseline.

One stem and one arch. Everything that goes wrong with n is a miscount of that arch.

Cursive n vs Cursive m

This is the single most misread pair in cursive. An m is one stem and two arches; an n is one stem and one arch. Written quickly the arches lose definition and become a row of identical bumps, at which point the reader is counting humps rather than recognising a letter — and miscounting. Slowing down on the arch tops is the only real fix.

Joining n to the Next Letter

The n exits on the baseline and joins into everything without trouble. The hard sequences are the ones that stack arches: min, nun, manner. When several arch letters run together the word turns into an unreadable picket fence, and the cure is spacing rather than shape.

Common Mistakes with Cursive N

  • Two arches. That is an m.
  • A tall stem. An n with an ascender is an h.
  • Rounded arch tops in a run of letters. Keeps humps countable if they stay slightly pointed.
  • Ending high. The n exits on the baseline.

Cursive N FAQ

What is the cursive N?

The cursive N is the joined-up form of the letter N. The capital keeps the printed diagonal between two full-height stems; the lowercase is a short stem plus one arch inside the x-height, exiting on the baseline. Online, “cursive N” also means the copy-and-paste characters (𝒩 and 𝓃) above.

How do you write n in cursive?

In short: up to x-height, down to the baseline, push into one arch, come down and exit. Two downstrokes total. Steps and the diagram are above.

What is the difference between a cursive n and a cursive m?

Arch count: n has one, m has two. Nothing else differs, which is why the two are the most commonly confused letters in joined-up writing.

Why is my cursive unreadable in words like ‘minimum’?

Because n, m, u, i and v are all built from the same arch and valley shapes, and a word made of them becomes a row of identical bumps. Add slightly more space between letters and keep the arch tops pointed rather than rounded.

Can I copy and paste a cursive N?

Yes. Every N above is a Unicode character rather than an image, so a click copies real text into any field that accepts typing. The Double-Struck capital (ℕ) comes from the Letterlike Symbols block, but pastes the same as the rest.

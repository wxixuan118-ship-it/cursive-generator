Cursive M Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital M in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and carry a stroke up to cap height.
  2. Come back down to the baseline for the first valley.
  3. Rise to cap height and drop again for the second valley.
  4. Rise a third time, then come down and exit right.

Three peaks, two valleys — the capital M is the widest letter in the alphabet and needs the horizontal room.

How to Write a Lowercase m in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and rise to x-height.
  2. Come down to the baseline.
  3. Push up into the first arch and back down.
  4. Push up into the second arch, come down and exit along the baseline.

One stem and two arches, which means three downstrokes in total. Counting them is how you keep m and n apart.

Cursive m vs Cursive n

The most misread pair in joined-up writing. An m is a stem plus two arches; an n is a stem plus one. Written at speed the arches lose their shape and become a row of identical bumps, and the reader ends up counting humps rather than recognising a letter — then miscounting. Words like minimum and numb are where this breaks down first.

Joining m to the Next Letter

The m exits on the baseline and joins into anything. The problem is stacking: m, n, u, i and v are all built from the same arches and valleys, so a word made of them turns into a picket fence. The fix is horizontal space between letters rather than any change to the letterforms.

Common Mistakes with Cursive M

  • One arch. That is an n.
  • Rounded arch tops. Keeping them slightly pointed is what makes humps countable.
  • Uneven arch widths. A narrow second arch reads as an i tucked onto an n.
  • A tall stem. The m starts at x-height, not the ascender.

Cursive M FAQ

What is the cursive M?

The cursive M is the joined-up form of the letter M. The capital is three peaks at cap height with two valleys on the baseline; the lowercase is a short stem plus two arches inside the x-height. Online, “cursive M” also means the copy-and-paste characters (ℳ and 𝓂) above.

How do you write m in cursive?

In short: up to x-height, down to the baseline, then two arches, each rising to x-height and coming back down. Three downstrokes in total, exiting on the baseline. Steps and the diagram are above.

What is the difference between a cursive m and a cursive n?

Arch count: m has two, n has one. Nothing else differs, which is why they are the most commonly confused letters in cursive.

Why is a word like ‘minimum’ so hard to read in cursive?

Because m, n, u and i are all built from the same arch and valley strokes. A word made almost entirely of them becomes a row of identical bumps with no shape cues to break it up. Extra letter spacing helps more than neater letters.

Can I copy and paste a cursive M?

Yes. The Cursive Script capital (ℳ) comes from the Letterlike Symbols block rather than the Mathematical Script block, one of eight capitals Unicode encoded there first, but it pastes like any other character.

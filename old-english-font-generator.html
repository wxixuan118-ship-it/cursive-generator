Style guide

Old English Font Styles

Blackletter is the one decorative style Unicode covers properly — two full alphabets, capitals and lowercase. These five groups build on that base.

Fraktur

The standard blackletter weight, and the closest thing Unicode has to a complete Old English alphabet. Angular strokes, forked terminals and almost no round shapes. Every letter A–Z and a–z exists.

Bold Fraktur

The same forms at a heavier weight, with tighter counters. It is the more legible of the two at small sizes because the strokes separate more clearly.

Ornate capitals

Blackletter capitals framed with manuscript ornaments — fleurons, fleurs-de-lis and asterisms. This mirrors how blackletter was actually set: a decorated opening letter carrying the line.

Medieval

The adjacent registers a manuscript page also used — small caps for chapter headings, even-width letters for annotation, outline forms for carved and inscribed text.

Mixed

Gothic-adjacent styles that are not strictly blackletter: heavy serif, gothic italic and dense script. Useful when true Fraktur is too hard to read for the length of text you have.

Old English, Blackletter and Medieval Text

The three terms get used interchangeably online, but they are not the same thing, and the difference explains why the styles on this page look the way they do.

Old English, strictly, is a language — the Anglo-Saxon spoken in England before roughly 1150. It was written in insular script, not blackletter. What people mean by “Old English font” today is the lettering style used on newspaper mastheads, diplomas and certificates: blackletter.

Blackletter is the letterform family itself, in use across Europe from about 1150 onwards. It is called blackletter because the dense vertical strokes and narrow spacing make a page look markedly darker than one set in roman type.

Fraktur is one specific branch of blackletter, developed in sixteenth-century Germany and the standard German printing type until the twentieth century. It is the branch Unicode encoded, which is why every style here descends from it.

Medieval lettering is broader still, covering the whole manuscript page: the blackletter body text, the decorated opening capital, the small caps used for headings and the ornaments between sections. The Medieval and Ornate categories on this page cover those companion registers rather than the body text alone.

That distinction also sets the boundary between this page and our gothic font generator. Where to Use Old English Text

Blackletter carries strong associations — formality, age, authority — which makes it very good in some places and wrong in others:

  • Headings and titles — a chapter heading, a channel name or a section title where one line of dense text is the point.
  • Certificates and awards — the register blackletter has occupied for centuries; it reads as formal without extra effort.
  • Social display names and usernames — supported almost everywhere, so it survives fields where fonts cannot be changed.
  • Monograms and single initials — use the A–Z chart to copy one decorated capital on its own.
  • Band, project and server names — short names where density and presence matter more than fast reading.
  • Historical and fantasy writing — chapter openers and place names in game documents, campaigns and worldbuilding notes.

Readability and Accessibility

Blackletter is the least readable body-text style in common use, and that is a design fact rather than an opinion — the vertical strokes are nearly identical between letters, so readers distinguish characters by small differences at the top and bottom. A few practical consequences:

  • Keep it to one line. Headings, names and titles work. Paragraphs do not, and long blackletter passages are genuinely difficult for many readers.
  • Some letters are easily confused. In lowercase Fraktur, 𝔫 and 𝔯, and 𝔠 and 𝔢, are close enough to misread at small sizes. Bold Fraktur separates them better.
  • Screen readers struggle. These characters are technically mathematical symbols, so assistive technology often reads them letter by letter or skips them. Keep essential information in plain text alongside.
  • No digits, no accents. Unicode's Fraktur ranges cover A–Z and a–z only. Numbers, punctuation and accented letters pass through unchanged, which is why a date inside a blackletter line looks mixed.
  • Search will not match it. A blackletter username will not come up in a plain-text search, so keep handles in normal letters.

Old English Font Generator FAQ

What is an Old English font generator?

It converts typed text into blackletter — the dense, angular lettering used on mastheads, certificates and diplomas. The output is Unicode characters rather than an image or a font file, so it stays styled when you paste it into a bio, username, message or document.

Is Old English the same as blackletter?

In everyday use, yes. Strictly, Old English is a language written in insular script, while blackletter is the letterform family used across Europe from about 1150. What people mean by an Old English font is blackletter, and specifically Fraktur, which is the branch Unicode encoded.

Does Old English text work on Instagram, TikTok and Discord?

Yes, and it is one of the most reliable decorative styles for all three. Unicode has complete Fraktur alphabets in both weights, so no letters drop out. It works in bios, captions, comments, display names and server nicknames.

Why do some capitals look different from the others?

Five blackletter capitals — C, H, I, R and Z — come from Unicode's letterlike-symbols block rather than the main Fraktur block, a legacy of how mathematical notation was encoded. They are correct blackletter characters, but a few older fonts style them slightly differently from their neighbours.

Can I use numbers in Old English font?

Not in the blackletter styles. Unicode's Fraktur ranges cover A–Z and a–z only, so digits pass through unchanged. If you need styled numbers alongside blackletter text, the Carved Outline, Scribe Mono and Medieval Wide styles on this page do include them.

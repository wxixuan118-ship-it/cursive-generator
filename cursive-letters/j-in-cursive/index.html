Cursive J Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital J in Cursive

  1. Start below cap height with a wide hook curling left.
  2. Carry the stroke down through the x-height.
  3. Continue past the baseline into the descender.
  4. Loop left and return to the baseline.

The capital J is one of the capitals that reaches below the baseline, which is why it needs the descender space of the line it sits on.

How to Write a Lowercase j in Cursive

  1. Start at x-height and pull straight down through the baseline.
  2. Continue into the descender.
  3. Loop left and come back up to the baseline to exit.
  4. Finish the word, then place the dot above the stroke.

Like the i, the j takes its dot after the word is finished, not as you write the letter.

Cursive j vs Cursive g

Both drop a left-swinging loop below the baseline, so the descenders are effectively the same stroke. The difference is above the line: a g has a closed round bowl at x-height, while the j is a single narrow stroke with a dot. If the dot is missing and your j is written with any curve at the top, the two become genuinely hard to separate.

Joining j to the Next Letter

The descender loop returns to the baseline, so the j joins forward normally — though in English it almost never needs to, since j is overwhelmingly a word-initial letter. The practical issue is vertical: a j sitting above an ascender letter on the next line collides with it, and the usual fix is to shorten the loop.

Common Mistakes with Cursive J

  • Dotting mid-word. The dot drifts onto the following letter.
  • A bowl at the top. Adding a curve at x-height turns the j into a g.
  • Looping the descender right. The j loop swings left.
  • A loop that does not return to the baseline. Nothing can join onto it.

Cursive J FAQ

What is the cursive J?

The cursive J is the joined-up form of the letter J. The capital is a wide hooked top that curves down through the baseline into a descender loop; the lowercase is a narrow stroke from x-height into the same loop, dotted afterwards. Online, “cursive J” also means the copy-and-paste characters (𝒥 and 𝒿) above.

How do you write j in cursive?

In short: a straight stroke from x-height down through the baseline, a loop swinging left, back up to the baseline to exit — then dot it once the word is done. Steps and the diagram are above.

When do you dot a cursive j?

After the whole word is written, exactly like an i. Dotting as you reach the letter interrupts the stroke and the dot tends to land over whatever comes next.

What is the difference between a cursive j and a cursive g?

The top. A g has a closed round bowl at x-height; a j is a single narrow stroke with a dot above it. The descender loops are the same.

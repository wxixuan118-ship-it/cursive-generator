Cursive L Stroke Order

How to Write a Capital L in Cursive

  1. Start near cap height with a small loop curling left.
  2. Carry the stroke down in a long curve towards the baseline.
  3. Swing left into a wide loop that sits on the baseline.
  4. Bring the stroke back out to the right to finish.

The bottom loop is what makes it an L. Without it the letter reads as a large comma.

How to Write a Lowercase l in Cursive

  1. Start on the baseline and rise to the ascender line, leaving a narrow loop.
  2. Come back down the same path to the baseline.
  3. Keep the loop open — it should be visible, not a single line.
  4. Exit right along the baseline.

Height is everything here: the l is a full ascender loop, and anything shorter is read as an e.

Cursive l vs Cursive e

They are the same shape at two different scales — a loop rising from the baseline. The l reaches the ascender line; the e stays inside the x-height. There is nothing else to tell them apart, which is why cramped handwriting turns tell into tee and feel into fell. If your letters are small, exaggerate the l.

Joining l to the Next Letter

The l exits on the baseline and joins cleanly into everything. The difficulty is doubling: ll appears constantly in English and two identical loops side by side blur into one tall mark. Keeping the second loop slightly narrower than the first is the usual fix.

Common Mistakes with Cursive L

  • A closed loop. A filled-in l is a straight line and reads as a t without its bar.
  • Insufficient height. An l that stops short of the ascender is an e.
  • Identical double l. Vary the width slightly so the pair stays countable.
  • Starting at the top. The l is written upward from the baseline.

Cursive L FAQ

What is the cursive L?

The cursive L is the joined-up form of the letter L. The capital is a long curving stroke finished with a wide loop along the baseline; the lowercase is a tall narrow loop from the baseline to the ascender line and back, exiting on the baseline. Online, “cursive L” also means the copy-and-paste characters (ℒ and 𝓁) above.

How do you write l in cursive?

In short: one upward stroke from the baseline to the ascender line, leaving a visible loop, then straight back down to the baseline and out to the right. Keep the loop open. Steps and diagram are above.

What is the difference between a cursive l and a cursive e?

Height only. Both are loops rising from the baseline; the l reaches the ascender line and the e stops inside the x-height. Small handwriting makes them interchangeable, so the l has to be drawn tall.

Why do double l's look wrong in cursive?

Because two identical loops merge into one shape and the reader cannot count them. Make the second loop a little narrower than the first and ll stays legible.

Can I copy and paste a cursive L?

Yes. The Cursive Script capital (ℒ) comes from the Letterlike Symbols block rather than the Mathematical Script block, one of the eight capitals Unicode encoded there first. It pastes normally.
